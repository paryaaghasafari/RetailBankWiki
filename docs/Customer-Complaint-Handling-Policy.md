# Customer Complaint Handling Policy

> Fictional policy for the Northvale Demo Bank GenAI RAG capstone demo. Do not use as real customer-service guidance.

## Summary

This policy defines how Northvale Demo Bank ("the Bank") receives, acknowledges, investigates, resolves, and reports customer complaints across every channel — branch, phone, online banking, mobile app, mailed correspondence, social media direct message, and regulatory referral. A complaint is any expression of dissatisfaction by a Customer about a product, service, fee, employee, or process at the Bank, whether oral or written, that requires a response. The policy applies to every Bank employee who interacts with Customers, with first-line accountability sitting with the Customer Service Representative (CSR) who takes the complaint and second-line accountability sitting with the Branch Manager (for branch complaints) or the Customer Operations Lead (for remote-channel complaints). The Bank shall acknowledge every complaint within five (5) business days of receipt. The Bank targets resolution within thirty (30) calendar days. Complaints not resolved within sixty (60) calendar days trigger Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) tracking — the complaint is escalated to the Office of the General Counsel and a separate CFPB-formatted response is prepared in case the customer files with the regulator. Complaints that, in the course of investigation, surface suspected money laundering, third-party fraud, or other financial crime are referred to the Anti-Money-Laundering (AML) Procedures and become the trigger for a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) filed within thirty (30) calendar days of detection (extendable to sixty (60) days if no suspect is identified). The customer-facing complaint resolution and the back-end SAR filing run in parallel and the customer is not informed that a SAR has been filed. The policy is restated in Chapter 5 of the Compliance Handbook v8 and is the source of the customer-service metrics published in the Q1 2026 Quarterly Compliance Update.

## Definitions

- **Complaint**: Any expression of dissatisfaction by a Customer about a product, service, fee, employee, or process that requires a response.
- **CSR (Customer Service Representative)**: The first-line employee who logs the complaint into the Complaint Management System (CMS).
- **CMS (Complaint Management System)**: The Bank's case-management tool of record for complaints.
- **Acknowledgement**: The Bank's written or oral confirmation to the Customer that the complaint has been received and a case opened.
- **Resolution**: A documented outcome communicated to the Customer — either substantive remedy, a defensible decline, or a referral to another body.
- **CFPB**: Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, the U.S. regulator that receives consumer complaints about financial products.
- **Regulatory Complaint**: A complaint received from the CFPB, a state regulator, or another official body referring a consumer complaint.
- **SAR Referral**: An internal escalation from this policy into the AML Procedures.
- **Service Recovery**: Any goodwill remedy (fee waiver, courtesy credit) used to resolve a complaint in the Bank's favour.

## 1. Intake and Acknowledgement

Every complaint, regardless of channel, shall be logged in the Complaint Management System (CMS) by the receiving employee within one (1) business day of receipt. The CSR shall capture: (a) the Customer's identity (account number where available), (b) the date and channel of receipt, (c) a verbatim description of the complaint, (d) the product, service, or fee at issue, (e) any monetary amount in dispute, and (f) any deadline or external date constraint (e.g., the date by which a CFPB referral expects a response).

The Bank shall acknowledge the complaint to the Customer within five (5) business days of receipt. Acknowledgement may be by the same channel as the complaint or by a higher-trust channel (e.g., a phone complaint may be acknowledged by an emailed case-confirmation letter). The acknowledgement shall include the CMS case number, the named case owner, and the expected resolution timeline.

### Key takeaways

- Every complaint is logged in CMS within 1 business day.
- The acknowledgement SLA is 5 business days from receipt.
- The acknowledgement includes case number, owner, and timeline.
- Every channel is treated identically once the complaint is in CMS.

## 2. Investigation and Resolution

The case owner — usually a CSR Lead or a Branch Manager — investigates the complaint by reviewing transaction logs, employee notes, recorded calls (where available), and any documentary evidence the Customer has provided. The Bank shall target resolution within thirty (30) calendar days of receipt. Resolution may be (a) a substantive remedy (refund, fee waiver, account adjustment, written apology, employee coaching), (b) a defensible decline communicated to the Customer with reasons, or (c) a referral to another body (e.g., to AML, to the credit team, or to a third-party service provider).

The 30-calendar-day target is achieved on the vast majority of complaints. The Q1 2026 Quarterly Compliance Update reports an average resolution time of 18 calendar days across the Bank's complaint book. Complex complaints — those involving multiple transactions, multiple Bank employees, or an external party — may exceed 30 calendar days; in those cases the case owner shall send a written status update to the Customer at day 30 and at every subsequent 30-day mark until resolution.

### Key takeaways

- The resolution target is 30 calendar days from receipt.
- Resolution can be substantive remedy, defensible decline, or referral.
- Status updates are issued every 30 days for complex complaints.
- The Q1 2026 Bank-wide average is 18 calendar days.

### Related Procedures

Complaints that escalate to CFPB tracking after day 60 follow Section 3.

## 3. CFPB-Tracked Escalation

Complaints not resolved within sixty (60) calendar days of receipt trigger CFPB tracking. The case is escalated to the Office of the General Counsel, who reviews the file and prepares a CFPB-formatted response in case the Customer files with the regulator. The Bank also pre-emptively notifies the Customer that the resolution timeline has been exceeded and provides a revised expected date.

The Bank receives a small but steady volume of complaints directly from the CFPB Consumer Complaint Database. Regulatory Complaints follow the same intake and resolution flow as direct complaints but with two procedural differences: (a) the acknowledgement is sent to both the Customer and the CFPB within 15 calendar days of the CFPB referral, per the CFPB's portal requirements, and (b) the Office of the General Counsel is the case owner from day one rather than a CSR Lead.

### Key takeaways

- CFPB tracking begins at day 60 from complaint receipt.
- The Office of the General Counsel takes ownership at CFPB-tracking escalation.
- The Customer is notified pre-emptively of timeline overruns.
- Regulatory Complaints have a 15-calendar-day CFPB acknowledgement deadline.

### Related Procedures

CFPB-tracked complaints often surface compliance or risk issues that are then routed to compliance, the credit team, or AML.

## 4. Complaints That Reveal Suspected Financial Crime

A material portion of complaints, particularly those involving disputed transactions or perceived unauthorised activity, can surface patterns that suggest financial crime — third-party fraud, account takeover, money laundering through the Customer's accounts, or insider misconduct. When the case owner identifies such a pattern, the complaint is referred to the AML team within two (2) business days of identification.

Once received by AML, the case enters the L1 / L2 escalation workflow described in the [AML Procedures](AML-Anti-Money-Laundering-Procedures.md). If reasonable suspicion is established, a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) is filed with FinCEN within thirty (30) calendar days of detection (extendable to sixty (60) days if no suspect has been identified). The SAR filing window cited here is identical to the window cited in the AML Procedures — both pages use the canonical 30 / 60 day figures. The customer-facing complaint resolution and the back-end SAR filing run in parallel. The Customer is not informed that a SAR has been filed; the Bank is prohibited from disclosing the existence of a SAR to the subject of the report.

| Touchpoint | Customer-Facing Track | Internal SAR Track |
|---|---|---|
| Acknowledgement (5 business days) | Standard CMS acknowledgement | Internal AML referral logged |
| Investigation (30 calendar days) | Bank investigates the transaction | AML L1 / L2 build case file |
| Resolution | Customer told the outcome of the dispute | SAR filed 30 / 60 days from detection |
| Communication | Resolution letter to Customer | No disclosure to Customer about SAR |

The takeaway is that complaint-sourced SARs are common and the workflow is bifurcated — the Customer experiences a standard 30-day complaint resolution and is unaware of the SAR. The Bank uses the same 30 / 60 day SAR filing window cited in the AML Procedures and Chapter 4 of the Compliance Handbook v8.

### Key takeaways

- Complaints suggesting financial crime are referred to AML within 2 business days.
- AML follows its standard L1 / L2 workflow.
- SAR filing window is 30 / 60 calendar days (same as AML Procedures).
- Customer is never informed about the SAR filing.

### Related Procedures

The full AML L1 / L2 workflow and the BSA Officer sign-off are documented in [AML-Anti-Money-Laundering-Procedures.md](AML-Anti-Money-Laundering-Procedures.md), Section 2.

## 5. Severity Matrix and Remedies

Complaints are categorised by severity at intake to drive case routing and remedy authority. The table below sets out the four severity levels, the typical issues at each level, and the remedy authority delegated to each role.

| Severity | Typical Issues | Remedy Authority |
|---|---|---|
| S1 (Low) | One-off fee dispute under $50; service-quality concern | CSR may waive up to $50 per case |
| S2 (Medium) | Multiple fees; minor process error; missed callback | CSR Lead may waive up to $250 per case |
| S3 (High) | Material monetary loss to Customer; product mis-selling | Branch Manager may approve up to $2,500 per case |
| S4 (Critical) | Suspected fraud; data privacy breach; safety issue | Customer Operations Lead and Office of the General Counsel |

In summary, the Bank's complaint remedy framework is tiered — small goodwill credits sit with the CSR, mid-sized remediation sits with the CSR Lead, large customer-loss remediation sits with the Branch Manager, and any complaint with regulatory or safety implications escalates to the Customer Operations Lead and Counsel. The $35 returned-item fee and the 15-day late-payment grace cited in the Credit Policy Manual are the most-disputed retail fee items and typically resolve at S1 or S2.

### Key takeaways

- S1 to S4 severity ladder maps to remedy authority.
- CSR remedy ceiling is $50; CSR Lead is $250; Branch Manager is $2,500.
- S4 complaints route to Customer Operations Lead and Counsel.
- Returned-item and late-payment fee disputes are the most common S1 / S2 cases.

### Related Procedures

The underlying late-payment grace period (15 calendar days) and returned-item fee ($35) are documented in the [Credit Policy Manual](attachments/credit-policy-manual.pdf), Section 6.

## Common Scenarios

**Scenario A — Simple fee dispute.** A Customer calls in to dispute a $35 returned-item fee from a payment posted on the same day a deposit cleared. The CSR confirms the timing, agrees the fee is hard to defend, and waives it at S1 authority. The case is logged, acknowledged with a confirmation email, and closed the same day. Resolution time: less than one business day.

**Scenario B — Unauthorised transaction complaint.** A Customer reports two unauthorised debit-card transactions totalling $620. The CSR opens the case as S3, files the dispute with the card network, and provides a provisional credit within five business days. The investigation completes at day 22; both transactions are confirmed unauthorised, the provisional credit becomes permanent, and the Customer is notified. Resolution time: 22 calendar days.

**Scenario C — Complaint revealing fraud pattern.** A Customer complains about a series of small recurring debits she doesn't recognise. The CSR notices that other Customers have filed similar complaints citing the same merchant ID over the past month. The CSR escalates to S3 and refers the case to AML the same day. AML's L2 investigator confirms a third-party fraud ring. A SAR is filed with FinCEN at day 26 from detection. The Customer is reimbursed for the disputed transactions at day 28 and her complaint is closed. She is never informed about the SAR.

**Scenario D — Complaint received via CFPB.** A Customer files a complaint via the CFPB Consumer Complaint Database alleging a mortgage servicing error. The CFPB forwards the complaint to the Bank. The Office of the General Counsel acknowledges to both the CFPB and the Customer within 14 calendar days. The investigation completes at day 35 and the response is filed with both parties. Resolution time: 35 calendar days; CFPB tracking active throughout.

**Scenario E — Complex complaint exceeding 30 days.** A Customer complains about being charged investment-advisory fees on an account he says he closed. Investigation requires pulling six years of statements and reviewing two employees' notes. At day 30 the case owner sends a written status update with a revised target of day 55. The investigation completes at day 49 with full refund of fees. The case closes; no CFPB tracking is triggered because resolution is before day 60.

### Key takeaways

- Simple fee disputes resolve at S1 in under a day.
- Disputed transactions are S3 and typically resolve within 30 days with provisional credit.
- Fraud-pattern complaints trigger an AML referral and parallel SAR.
- CFPB-sourced complaints follow a special acknowledgement track.
- Complex complaints exceed 30 days but typically resolve before 60.

### Related Procedures

The AML L1 / L2 path for Scenario C is in [AML-Anti-Money-Laundering-Procedures.md](AML-Anti-Money-Laundering-Procedures.md).

## FAQ

**Q1. How long does the Bank have to acknowledge a complaint?**
5 business days from receipt.

**Q2. What's the Bank's target resolution time?**
30 calendar days from receipt.

**Q3. When does CFPB tracking kick in?**
At 60 calendar days from receipt, if the complaint is still unresolved.

**Q4. Where are complaints logged?**
In the Complaint Management System (CMS) within 1 business day of receipt by any channel.

**Q5. What happens if a complaint reveals suspected fraud or money laundering?**
The case is referred to the AML team within 2 business days of identification. The AML team follows the standard L1 / L2 workflow and may file a SAR within 30 calendar days of detection (60 days if no suspect identified).

**Q6. Is the customer informed about a SAR filing?**
No. The Bank is prohibited from disclosing the existence of a SAR to the subject of the report. The customer-facing complaint is resolved separately under this policy.

**Q7. What can a CSR waive without escalation?**
Up to $50 per case (S1 authority).

**Q8. What can a Branch Manager waive without further escalation?**
Up to $2,500 per case (S3 authority).

**Q9. What's a Regulatory Complaint?**
A complaint received from the CFPB, a state regulator, or another official body. It follows the same flow but with the Office of the General Counsel as case owner and a 15-day acknowledgement deadline to the regulator.

**Q10. What's the most-disputed retail fee?**
The $35 returned-item fee and late-payment fees. These are the most common S1 / S2 cases.

**Q11. What's the 15-day grace period for late payments?**
The grace period documented in the Credit Policy Manual — payments made within 15 calendar days of the due date are not charged a late fee. Complaints arising after day 15 typically resolve at S1 or S2.

**Q12. Where is this policy formally documented for audit?**
Chapter 5 of the Compliance Handbook v8 (attached). Q1 2026 conformance metrics are in the Quarterly Compliance Update.
